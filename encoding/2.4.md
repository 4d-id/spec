## Scope and precedence

Parts 1 through 5 define the normative abstract requirements; Part 6 and this document are informative. This versioned pointer describes the artifacts actually present, not a promise that all abstract records or operations have a complete wire encoding. If a schema accepts a record that violates a normative requirement, acceptance by the schema does not make that record conformant. This document adds no normative tests to the 112-entry manifest.

The boundary is OBSERVE -> GROUND -> IDENTIFY -> RESOLVE -> INTEROPERATE. Observations are evidence. External grounding determines the referent, localization determines where, and 4D-ID preserves which. SEGS is adjacent. The bindings record externally supplied evidence, candidate associations, registrations, accepted state, and attributed decisions; they do not implement perception, matching, registration, localization, or rendering. An acceptable existing identity is reused before minting.

## Registration binding

`state.parent` is the sole composition edge and `state.pose` is relative to it. `state.registrations` may be absent, empty, or contain many independent externally supplied records. Each registration has:

- `frame`: target frame 4D-ID.
- Optional `transform`: entity-local to target-frame position/orientation, with optional positive uniform scale. Missing is unknown, not identity.
- `tier`: `logical`, `navigational`, or `metric`; `confidence` in [0, 1].
- `valid_from` and `valid_to`: a half-open validity interval, with end later than start.
- `provenance`: external producer, method, evidence references, assertion time (`registered`), and RMS when known. Metric claims require RMS. If tier is repeated in provenance, it must agree with the registration tier. Missing RMS is unknown, not zero.
- `status`: `proposed`, `accepted`, `rejected`, `disputed`, `superseded`, or `expired`.

Registrations never add parents or establish identity. Schema checks enforce field and transform shapes, not tree acyclicity, interval ordering, freshness, tier truth, external evidence, or authority. Multiple registrations can disagree without changing composition state. Only valid, accepted placement assertions are usable as current registration; resolving their disagreements remains external.

## Identifier and migration binding

The existing URI grammar already admits `id` as a variant. In this mode the `cell` slot holds an opaque non-spatial home token, not a grid cell; no vertical reference or genesis timestamp is allowed. A descriptor is UUIDv4 or documented high-entropy random material, not UUIDv7. The home token and descriptor encode no place, person, or time, including hashed derivatives. The schema checks the grammar and absence of forbidden suffixes; it cannot prove generation entropy, privacy, UUID version, or that arbitrary bytes are not derived from private data. Those checks remain normative `todo` inspection/functional work.

Keep legacy spatial IDs and references unchanged, including UUIDv7 implicit-genesis normalization and explicit-genesis forms. Spatial genesis fields remain mint/routing metadata, not grounding evidence or current location. Existing identities must not be reminted just to change mode. New privacy-sensitive identities can use `id` after valid bounded reconciliation; deployments publish/configure the non-spatial home-token mapping specified in 14.1. Current anchors and registrations determine location, not identifier bytes. `id` is not a valid spatial-anchor or domain-grid variant.

Consumers upgrading from 2.3 need to accept the optional registration array, the `id` restrictions, and the bounded resolve decision envelope. Existing references remain valid, but 2.4 does not promise that all 2.3 JSON records meet strengthened provenance requirements or that response envelopes are byte-for-byte unchanged. In 2.4, provenance identifies producer and method; registration provenance additionally requires evidence and assertion time. A deployment should negotiate its binding version explicitly. The larger identifier/grid design remains open for 2.5; this binding does not settle it.

## Reconciliation and authority binding

Decision outcomes are `reuse`, `no_acceptable_match`, `ambiguous`, `conflict`, and `rejected`. The schema requires `selected_id` only for reuse, and permits a no-acceptable-match outcome only with `lookup_complete: true` and `exhausted: false`. These fields assert what happened; schema validation cannot verify the lookup or its authority. Scope, policy, evidence/provenance, finite limits, decision time and expiry are mandatory. An incomplete, unavailable, truncated or exhausted lookup is not no-match. A negative is scoped and expiring, never global absence or mint authorization by itself. Candidate records and decision records alone cannot mutate identity or state.

The REST illustration maps reuse to HTTP 200 (the outer ID must equal the decision's selected ID), complete scoped no-match to 404, ambiguity/conflict to 409, rejection to 403, and unavailable/incomplete/exhausted lookup to 503 with a non-negative outcome. Other transport bindings may choose their own status mapping while preserving the five outcomes. Signature serialization for the signed negative required by Clause 10 is not yet specified here, so this OpenAPI file alone cannot demonstrate that requirement.

Authority strings and the conferred/overlapping/dispute records specified in 14.4 are attribution, not proof of permission. Authentication, authorization, delegation validation, and precedence policies remain deployment choices under Clause 12. This document defines no complete security scheme, grant/dispute wire schema, signature suite, or authority-management API. Those abstract normative tests remain `todo`.

## Partial conformance, explicitly bounded

The manifest contains exactly 112 normative tests: the existing 107 plus genesis privacy, bounded reconciliation, conferred authority, overlapping authority, and disputes. All five additions are `todo`. No manifest status means a normative test passed:

- `schema`: positive and negative fixtures are wired in `../conformance/shape_checks.py`. Each entry's `coverage` names the tested subset and important omissions. These are partial shape checks, not complete data/functional/inspection tests.
- `todo`: no complete normative implementation/test is wired here. Supplemental schema regressions may still cover some fields without changing this status.

Validation checks all schema syntax/metaschemas and references, OpenAPI structure and response-outcome constraints, the illustrative Part 1 state, and one-to-one requirement/Annex A/manifest linkage with exactly 112 unique entries. A successful run reports artifact assertions, not 112 conformance passes. The repository includes no runtime implementation.

Examples of uncovered obligations include UUID entropy and normalization, timestamp semantics, unit quaternion/covariance math, tree cycles/depth, registration validity ordering and identity invariance, complete fused/agent provenance, mandatory relation metadata and selection behavior, observation expiry/propagation, lookup completion and expiry enforcement, authentication, authority verification/disputes, domain-grid mathematics, and lifecycle effects. Existing JSON shapes deliberately remain partial rather than claiming these properties from parsing alone.

Binary encoding, signature canonicalization, complete extension/authority/lifecycle record schemas, stream framing, adapter bindings, and a full functional suite remain future work. A profile claim needs evidence for every applicable normative requirement, not just this validator's success.
